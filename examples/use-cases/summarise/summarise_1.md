**Role of NAMPT in Aging:**

Nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NAMPT) is a key enzyme in the NAD+ salvage pathway, responsible for converting nicotinamide to nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN), a precursor for NAD+ biosynthesis. NAD+ is a critical coenzyme involved in various cellular processes, including energy metabolism, DNA repair, and regulation of gene expression through sirtuins.

**Impact on Aging:**

Reduced NAD+ levels are associated with aging and age-related decline in cellular function. Decreased NAMPT activity leads to lower NAD+ availability, impairing mitochondrial function, reducing the efficiency of DNA repair mechanisms, and promoting cellular senescence. Conversely, maintaining or enhancing NAMPT activity can elevate NAD+ levels, supporting mitochondrial health, promoting DNA repair, and potentially delaying aging processes.

**Mechanistic Insights:**

- **Sirtuin Activation:** NAD+ is a substrate for sirtuins, a family of NAD+-dependent deacetylases that regulate longevity, metabolic homeostasis, and stress responses. NAMPT-mediated NAD+ biosynthesis sustains sirtuin activity, which declines with age.
- **Mitochondrial Function:** NAD+ is essential for mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation. Decline in NAD+ impairs mitochondrial respiration, leading to increased oxidative stress and cellular damage.
- **DNA Repair:** NAD+ is required for enzymes like PARPs involved in DNA repair. NAD+ depletion hampers repair capacity, contributing to genomic instability and aging.
