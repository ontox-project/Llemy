**Modulation of Core Pathways by Microenvironment (Inflammatory and Metabolic Factors)**

The provided Minerva Map data highlights numerous reactions where inflammatory mediators and metabolic signals influence key signaling pathways involved in cell growth, survival, immune response, and tissue remodeling. These interactions demonstrate how the microenvironment shapes cellular responses through complex signaling networks.

**Inflammatory Modulation:**
- Cytokines such as IL-1, IL-6, IL-12, IL-17, IL-18, IL-22, and TNF-alpha are recurrently shown to positively influence pathways like NF-κB, JAK-STAT, and MAPK cascades (e.g., reactions RID: 88662, 88717, 88957, 89116, 89444, 89668, 89889, 90643, 91194). These pathways regulate gene expression related to inflammation, cell proliferation, apoptosis, and tissue remodeling.
- Activation of NF-κB components (RELA, NFKB1, RELB, NFKB2) by inflammatory stimuli leads to transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL6, IL8, IL11, IL17F) and adhesion molecules (e.g., ICAM1, VCAM1), promoting immune cell recruitment and tissue inflammation (e.g., RIDs: 88723, 88922, 89466, 91194).
- Lipoxins and resolvins exert inhibitory effects on pro-inflammatory signaling, such as Lipoxins' action on TNF-α and IL-8 pathways (e.g., RIDs: 89827, 90025), illustrating how resolution mediators modulate inflammation.

**Metabolic Modulation:**
- Metabolites like arachidonic acid derivatives (e.g., prostaglandins, leukotrienes, lipoxins) influence inflammatory responses and tissue remodeling (e.g., reactions RID: 90127, 90217, 90244). These lipid mediators can activate or inhibit core signaling pathways, affecting processes like vasodilation, cell migration, and fibrosis.
- Amino acids such as arginine and ornithine are involved in nitric oxide (NO) synthesis and polyamine production, which regulate vascular tone, immune cell function, and cell proliferation (e.g., reactions RID: 88880, 89270, 92766). NO acts as a signaling molecule modulating vasodilation and immune responses.
- Lipid signaling molecules (e.g., PtdIns(3,4,5)P3, PGE2, PGD2) modulate kinase cascades like PI3K-AKT and MAPK pathways, influencing cell survival, proliferation, and barrier function (e.g., reactions RID: 91157, 91254).

**Integration and Core Pathway Modulation:**
- Inflammatory cytokines activate JAK-STAT pathways (notably JAK1, JAK2, TYK2) leading to transcription of cytokines, chemokines, and survival genes (e.g., reactions RID: 88669, 88910, 89445). These signals promote immune cell proliferation, differentiation, and inflammatory mediator production.
- Activation of MAPK pathways (ERK, JNK, p38) by cytokines and stress signals (e.g., reactions RID: 91194, 91209, 91400) results in transcription factors like FOS, JUN, and ELK1, which regulate genes involved in inflammation, cell migration, and tissue remodeling.
- Metabolic intermediates such as cyclic AMP (cAMP) and calcium ions (Ca2+) modulate kinase activity (PKA, CAMK2) and transcription factors, further influencing core pathways (e.g., reactions RID: 91168, 91245).

**Summary:**
The data underscores that the microenvironment—rich in inflammatory cytokines, lipid mediators, and metabolic signals—modulates core pathways such as NF-κB, JAK-STAT, and MAPK. These interactions regulate immune responses, cell proliferation, apoptosis, and tissue remodeling, which are central to inflammation, fibrosis, and disease progression, notably in conditions like asthma and cancer.
